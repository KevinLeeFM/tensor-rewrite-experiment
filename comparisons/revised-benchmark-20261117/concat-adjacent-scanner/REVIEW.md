## Source and model comparison

The pinned XLA source (`e58e523b11f1fde802a493a776d158007486e1bf`) performs the scan in `algebraic_simplifier.cc:2162–2229`. For a pending unit-stride slice it carries the original source instruction and the current end on the concat axis. It consumes the next operand only when that operand is a unit-stride Slice, has the same source instruction, starts exactly at the pending end, and has the same starts as the first slice on all other axes. It updates the pending end to the candidate's exclusive limit. On the first failed condition it breaks without consuming that candidate; the outer loop then starts a new scan at that candidate. A non-slice or non-unit-stride slice is forwarded as one operand and advances the loop, so following candidates are considered afresh. The replacement uses the first slice's attributes and the last end, and the concat is replaced only if the operand count shrinks.

The Lean `Key (src, pre, post)` captures source identity and the non-concat crop origins, `slice` denotes unit stride, and `opaque` carries an arbitrary operand's values and width. `merge` tests exactly key equality and touching intervals; its result preserves the first start and uses `(last exclusive end) - first start`. The proof's `pairLaw` establishes this is `w + v` and that the row values concatenate. `run` flushes the pending operand on a failed guard and recurses on the candidate, matching the source's break/outer-loop behavior. Its induction proves both total selected extent and value preservation. `maximal` separately proves every adjacent output boundary fails the actual merge guard. Thus “maximal” here means no adjacent output pair can undergo this same scanner merge; the proof does not assume a supplied merge sequence or assume every block belongs to a merge.

Opaque barriers are handled completely by the recursive definition: a slice cannot merge with an opaque block, the pending slice is flushed, the opaque block is retained, and the scan proceeds over later blocks. The proof is over arbitrary lists, so it also covers a new compatible run beginning immediately after a barrier, even though the concrete `ops` fixture does not contain a mergeable pair immediately after its opaque item. Cross-source or cross-crop candidates similarly fail `Key` equality; a later candidate is still reconsidered as a new pending item. The fixtures exercise three-way merging, distinct source IDs, distinct crop origins, a gap, an opaque barrier, a false crop merge, zero width and empty frames. They check an actual multi-row/multi-column packed value example. They are controls, not the general argument; the generic inductive theorems establish the arbitrary-list cases.

The source scanner is only reached after earlier `HandleConcatenate` paths: zero-element operands are filtered and cause an early return, layout-sensitive mode returns before scanning, and a same-axis nested concatenate is flattened and returned before scanning. Therefore packet cases with zero-width/empty-frame operands are valid for the total Lean scanner but may be handled before this source loop; and the source link applies to the post-normalization, layout-insensitive scanner subcase. Static legal HLO inputs also constrain slice rank, starts, limits and bounds, whereas the Lean readers are total and the theorem proves a stronger extensional identity without those bounds. The helper declaration at `hlo_instruction_utils.h:40–43` explicitly documents `IsUnstridedSlice` as requiring unit stride in every dimension (inspected header SHA-256 `aee1afeccd0d3c39346af7b0e772802c4ea96fc4ae7ad5e6b30d5a0be6d5beca`); its implementation body is not present in this source snapshot. These source dispatch and validity conditions are limitations on interpreting the theorem as a reachable handler branch, not gaps in the total list theorem.
