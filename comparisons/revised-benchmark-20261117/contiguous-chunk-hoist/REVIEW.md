## Source-to-model mapping

I inspected `ChunkSpec.lean`, all proof/control files, `FREEZE.md`, the replay script, `algebraic_simplifier.cc`, and the `CommonFactors` declaration contract in `xla/util.h` (all packet SHA entries verified). `FindContiguousChunks` partitions `CommonFactors(reshape result dims, transpose result dims)` intervals. It rejects any interval consuming anything other than exactly one reshape-result axis, rejects insertion of a unit axis (`num_transpose_dims == 0`), and requires the transpose dimension numbers inside each interval to ascend contiguously by 1. For a successful interval, the returned chunk's start is its first input-axis number, its width is the matching reshape-result extent, and `result_dim_idx` is the output position. The `CommonFactors` contract says aligned interval products agree. Because the transpose's dimensions are a permutation and each chunk is internally increasing and consecutive, the chunks form a partition of input axes into consecutive source-order groups when sorted by `start_dim`.

The caller sorts chunks by `start_dim`, sets new reshape dimensions to the sorted chunk widths, and assigns `new_transpose_dims[result_dim_idx] = sorted_chunk_index`. In the model, `p.toInput` maps an output-axis position to exactly that sorted input-block index; `p.toOutput` is its inverse. The model preserves the order of axes inside each block, as the source's contiguous-ascending predicate requires. Its `blocks k` lists are precisely each such input-order group, and their products are the matching outer reshape axis sizes. The emitted flat cell is the mixed-radix pack of one coordinate per sorted chunk. The original path packs the outer reshape coordinate, decodes its flat position over the complete transpose-output axis shape, and restores those digits in input-axis order. `expand`, `packBlocks`, and `directReshape` implement and prove that row-major relation.

`HandleReshape` only calls the helper in non-layout-sensitive mode with `enable_hoist_transpose_of_reshape()` true, after earlier empty-output, identity, reshape-chain, RNG, and bitcast/reshape-transpose-chain cases. The option has no enabled default in the inspected proto, so it is an explicit condition. The finite theorem assumes the helper has already succeeded and does not claim it succeeds for all shapes. Source zero-size preemption and the model's no-coordinate semantics agree: if any selected chunk product is zero, `Index` is uninhabited and there is no division by zero on a complete coordinate. Rank zero has the unique empty coordinate and pack value 0. Unit axes remain represented as radix-one digits.

The source snapshot includes the `CommonFactors` API contract and its call sites but no implementation body was found in this checkout. Thus this review checks the successful-return interpretation against the documented contract and `FindContiguousChunks` body, not independent execution or verification of the extractor implementation. The theorem is correctly labeled as a successful-partition parameterization. The inner operand is observed by its row-major flat offset; equal volume is required, and no physical layout claim follows.

## Statement, proof, controls, and overlap

The frozen goal is genuinely universal over arbitrary finite rank, block extents (including radix 1 and 0), inverse block orders, and all valid complete coordinates. It does not quantify over output coordinates for a zero total extent because no `Index` inhabitant exists. `expandPack` establishes digit bounds, `packBlocks` proves concatenated groups summarize to their single product radix, and `directReshape` independently decodes the outer packed coordinate before establishing the expanded-axis sequence. `verified` then equates the original and emitted inner-operand flat offsets. The explicit nonempty-list hypothesis mirrors that every successful source chunk consumes at least one transpose axis; it is unused in the arithmetic proof, while a zero extent makes the coordinate family empty.

The `[24] -> [2,3,4] -> [4,2,3] -> [4,6]` control has sorted input chunk widths `[6,4]` and replacement output-axis order `[1,0]`; coordinate `(3,1)` packs to 19, decodes to `(3,0,1)`, then inverse-transpose input digits `(0,1,3)`, whose flat offset is 7. Emitted sorted chunks also yield 7. The wrong-order control shows packing the unpermuted output coordinate reads 19; a separate 3-block rotation checks a non-self-inverse order (correct offset 20 versus incorrectly forward-mapped 6). Controls also cover a zero extent's uninhabited coordinate and radix-one/scalar pack behavior. They are small instance checks, while the theorem is general.

The source ledger previously listed `TryHoistTransposeOfReshape/contiguous-chunks` as unresolved and uncatalogued. The `HandleReshape/transpose-hoist-delegation` is a second lexical source label for this same terminal, not another mathematical case. Existing transpose composition, broadcast-preserved-factor, and held-out space-depth proofs have different graph equations; this proof does not close their source records. It is one new complete geometry-level mathematical case for the successful contiguous-chunk branch, with extractor and branch conditions disclosed.
