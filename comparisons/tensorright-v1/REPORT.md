# Lean comparison with the TensorRight paper artifact

## Answer and scope

**Yes, for this paper-era benchmark, an agent using Lean, existing proofs and the authors' rule descriptions was able to prove the modeled content of 116 of the 118 executed cases.** One further case has a concrete counterexample in the paper model; the remaining case is vacuous under the paper artifact's executed semantics. This is evidence of feasibility for this assisted, solution-informed task. It does not establish an autonomous success rate, a cost for a fresh agent, or correctness of the whole XLA pass.

A case here is one rule invoked by the paper artifact, with its own inputs, conditions and element types. The [case table](cases.md) links each to its artifact declaration, original result, Lean statements, conditions and explanation of the translation. The machine-readable [table](cases.json) contains the declarations and hashes. The code-to-model relationship is assumed, as in the requested comparison; we did not prove that the Lean statements or the paper DSL translate C++ correctly. The results do not make the C++ compiler bug-free.

| Paper artifact outcome | Lean model proved | Model counterexample | Paper-model success is vacuous | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Success | 114 | 0 | 1 | 115 |
| Timeout or solver failure | 2 | 1 | 0 | 3 |
| Total | 116 | 1 | 1 | 118 |

The workflow therefore did **not** reproduce 115 meaningful positive results: one reported success admits no valid inputs under the executed model. We proved both of the original failed remainder cases, `divmod/rule06` and `divmod/rule07`, using the artifact's guards and truncating integer remainder. For `dot/rule03`, the artifact constructs a right-hand side for Dot associativity but actually asks its verifier to check the left-hand side against itself. The Lean result proves the intended associativity, a stronger statement than the archived success. Both extra remainder proofs use existing project libraries; this is not a controlled comparison of proof-discovery time.

## What the workflow actually automated

The agent read the frozen rule descriptions, selected or wrote mathematical definitions, wrote and repaired Lean proofs using compiler feedback, and assigned checked statements to cases. Lean automatically checks those statements; Python checks inventory alignment, stored hashes, reported axioms and counts. The case-to-theorem assignments and translation explanations are agent-authored judgments, not outputs of a verified extractor or an independent semantic assessor. Some rules use general tensor operators; others use explicit bounded-coordinate formulas and supporting equivalences. There is no single new verifier that accepts an unseen C++ file and produces this coverage.

Reuse is a legitimate part of the feasibility demonstration, including proofs written earlier in this project. It does prevent treating the final comparison as 118 independent, fresh proof-discovery trials. The evidence supports agent-assisted proof construction under the adopted model assumptions; unattended generalization, comparative efficiency and reproducible success rates remain unmeasured.

## The two nonpositive model outcomes

- **`slice/rule13`: model counterexample.** A length-10 tensor sliced from 2 to 8 with stride 2 has a valid original expression; the proposed replacement starts at −3, which the artifact's Slice semantics rejects. Lean checks this witness, and the frozen TensorRight implementation confirms valid left and invalid right sides for Boolean, integer and real values when the parameters are fixed. See [the witness and reproduction](reverse-slice-witness.md). The unrestricted artifact reports failure, not a discovered XLA bug. We have not established whether XLA performs the same rewrite under these conditions.
- **`conv/rule01`: vacuous success.** Its input and original-kernel dilations are zero. Executed TensorRight convolution translates dilation `d` to interior padding `d − 1`, while Pad requires nonnegative interior padding; the spatial axis group is nonempty. Thus the original expression has no valid input. Lean checks the necessary arithmetic, and an unmodified artifact diagnostic reports that there are no valid inputs (or a symbolic invalidity) before printing success. The semantics explain the diagnostic; see [the evidence and reproduction](convolution-vacuity.md). This is not a proof of the intended convolution rewrite or a compiler bug.

There are no unclassified cases in this 118-case set. Neither nonpositive model outcome says anything by itself about a real C++ pass.

## A separately reproduced XLA bug

Before the paper-focused comparison, this project tested the actual algebraic simplifier at pinned XLA commit `e58e523b11f1fde802a493a776d158007486e1bf`. In the archived whole-pass host-evaluator test, for `x = [1, …, 10]`, an inner dynamic slice starting at 1 with length 6 is multiplied by 7 and then sliced at 2 with length 1. The original returns `[28]`; after the pass, the archived test returns `[4]`, because the multiplication is dropped. Input and output HLO verify; the no-pass control returns `[28]`. The archive includes controls and 33 runs, eight of which change values. This is a concrete finite-integer bug in that pinned pass, observed with the XLA host evaluator, not a compiled-backend test or a claim about current XLA. See the [prior report](../../reports/scaled-nested-slice-continuation.md) and [archived results](../../reports/scaled-nested-slice-replay-v1/result.json). It is separate from the paper-model Slice counterexample; no new compiler bug was reproduced during this comparison.

## How the results were checked

The target is the 118 rules actually invoked by the paper-era artifact, rather than all declarations or all rewrites in the current C++ file. The paper counted 175 rules, of which 121 were expressible and 118 implemented: 54 were unsupported and three expressible rules were unimplemented. The separately retained [newer candidate ledger](../../census/mathematical-ledger.json) has 488 entries, which are not a count of independently verified rules and are outside this primary denominator. This experiment does not establish coverage of the entire file.

We independently rebuilt the frozen paper sources in the authors' pinned Docker image and ran the suite offline. The image's prebundled sources differed from the frozen artifact, so the rebuild used the frozen sources. All 118 per-case success/failure outcomes matched the archive: 115 successes and three failures. The [rerun record](artifact-rerun.json), raw logs and [script](rerun_tensorright.sh) document the image, hashes, tools and one harmless printed-label shell-expansion exception. This rerun reproduces what the tool reported; the vacuity and Dot findings qualify what two reported successes actually establish.

A fresh Lean replay rebuilt 28 direct custom imports and their dependencies, then checked all 30 comparison modules in separate Lean processes. All passed, with 170 axiom reports limited to `propext`, `Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`; the replay detected no `sorryAx`, errors or warnings. The [audit](replay-audit.json) includes source and output hashes and the [build log](replay-build.log). Replay took 46.87 seconds on this host with at most three checks in parallel; this is a build/replay measurement, not proof-discovery time or a head-to-head performance comparison. Reproduce with `python3 comparisons/tensorright-v1/replay_all.py` from the repository root.

The comparison follows the artifact's stated types and conditions. Numeric examples include mathematical integers and exact reals; Boolean cases use Boolean values. Tensor shapes and bounded coordinates are modeled, and the proofs cover arbitrary shapes admitted by their statements. Specialized cases use the paper's preconditions and hints. For example, remainder follows the artifact's truncating remainder and zero-divisor behavior; grouped reductions and Dot are connected to the project's bounded tensor operations; signed padding accounts for negative edges. These checks support confidence in the modeled mathematics, while the mapping from artifact/C++ to Lean remains an assumption. No independent review of every new translation was performed. The artifact's vacuity and `lhs = lhs` findings show why even an automatically checked result still requires scrutiny of the statement.

IEEE rounding, NaNs, machine overflow, backends and layout are not established by an exact-real or unbounded-integer proof. An original artifact case involving infinity is modeled explicitly where applicable; that does not turn the broader result into an IEEE proof. The trusted base includes the Lean kernel and the pinned imported libraries, in addition to the stated translation assumption.

## Effort, reuse and limitations

The historical project baseline at `ab09a4e` contained **12,341 custom Lean code lines**, including work outside this paper comparison. The later entire project tree measured **13,072 lines**, again including unrelated work. The comparison directory adds **2,168 lines**, so the current broad tree plus comparison modules is **15,240 lines**. This is a snapshot of the custom code available, not a claim that all those lines were necessary or newly produced for these cases. It also does not measure human or agent time.

The transitive whole-module import closure for the 118 outcomes contains **6,966 custom Lean lines across 109 modules**: 2,168 comparison lines, 988 Pilot lines and 3,810 Theory lines. Shared modules are counted once; per-case closures overlap. The 2,168 include 45 lines in the two modules dedicated to the model counterexample and vacuity certificate, and the full closure includes 170 axiom-print commands. Some modules combine definitions, shared lemmas and rule proofs, so a reliable new partition into semantics, helpers, rule proofs, automation and certificates is **unknown**. The 6,966 is a whole-module upper bound, not a minimal proof size. External Lean, Std, Mathlib and FloatLib sources and non-Lean orchestration are excluded. [Dependency measurements](dependency-costs.json) include module hashes and per-case closures; reproduce with `python3 comparisons/tensorright-v1/measure_dependencies.py`.

The large historical investment and our reuse matter: the work used previous proofs, the paper's rule statements and reduction hints, and artifacts of earlier proof attempts. Available records do not reliably attribute agent time or count distinct bounded work blocks, and agent/model mixtures were not controlled. The earlier phase built substantially more infrastructure than was needed to begin case completion; prioritizing existing lemmas and actual cases subsequently completed the paper comparison. We did not measure the speedup or establish a causal comparison.

Lean's advantages here are reusable general proofs, independent kernel checking, explicit assumptions and the ability to validate concrete model counterexamples. The costs include building and maintaining tensor semantics and index lemmas, proof debugging, and reviewing translations. A proved exact-number model alone cannot promise correct executable floating-point code. The experiment demonstrates a viable assisted workflow under the paper's assumptions, plus two cases resolved beyond the paper's reported failures; it does not show that a fresh LLM can extract rules unaided, achieve this coverage on unseen passes, or do so more cheaply than TensorRight.
