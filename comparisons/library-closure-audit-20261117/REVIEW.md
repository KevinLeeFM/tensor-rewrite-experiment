# Paper-closure library inventory and targeted review

**Result:** all 109 frozen custom module paths are inventoried with matching SHA256 hashes and provisional roles; five additional high-fan-in modules received independent targeted semantic review. The audit found no hidden desired-output equality in those sampled declarations, but the remaining closure is **not certified clean**. This is a finite, read-only triage and sample, not a universal verification of the library.

## Inventory and role distinctions

[`inventory.json`](inventory.json) records one row per source from the historical `dependency-costs.json`, its frozen code-line count/hash, tentative role, module documentation and lexical theorem count, and direct syntactic `TensorRewrite` imports from maintained modules and the paper packet. Running `python3 comparisons/library-closure-audit-20261117/inventory.py` regenerates the JSON to stdout and fails if any frozen source hash differs. Reconciliation verified 109 distinct paths: 30 Pilot, 49 Theory, 30 paper-comparison case/witness modules.

The intentionally conservative module-name triage labels **12** generic-index/algebra infrastructure candidates, **25** rewrite-family/proof candidates, **42** operation/domain or mixed modules whose role remains unresolved, and **30** paper packet case/witness modules. These are categories for navigation, not an assertion that each module has one homogeneous role or a manually verified semantic classification. Each inventory row supplies its exact path and source documentation so mixed cases remain visible. The independently reviewed IndexMaps module, for example, contains general coordinate projection as well as HLO-specific Slice/Broadcast view constructors and is classified mixed rather than fully generic. In particular, a theorem legitimately proving a rewrite is a rewrite-specific proof even if it is rank-general and useful to several callers.

In the scanned `TensorRewrite/**/*.lean` and `comparisons/tensorright-v1/*.lean` sources, **76 of the 79 shared modules** have at least one direct maintained-tree importer, **28** have at least one direct paper-packet importer, and **25** have both. The three shared modules with no direct maintained-tree importer in that scan are `Pilot/ContractionRules.lean` (direct paper importers Dot and GroupedSums), `Theory/ArithmeticBroadcast.lean` (Broadcast), and `Theory/LogicalFamilyProof.lean` (Logical). The 30 paper comparison modules are experiment roots in the historical closure. Direct edges do not prove a declaration is used, do not establish that an importing maintained module is itself general-purpose, and do not cover arbitrary dynamically configured search paths or external consumers. No claim that a module is dead follows from no direct edge. Whole-module imports also differ from the transitive per-case dependencies in the original report.

## Selection and independent semantic reviews

The previous [seven-module held-out baseline review](../library-cleanliness-audit-20261117/REVIEW.md) already covered Tensor, Indexing, Permutation, ConstantGuard and three DynamicIndex modules. All seven occur in this 109-module inventory. Beyond them, we selected five modules with both semantically consequential geometry and high direct maintained-import counts: `Pilot/IndexMaps` (9), `Pilot/LinearIndexLemmas` (7), `Pilot/ReshapeLemmas` (7), `Theory/ConvolutionPointwiseLemmas` (7) and `Theory/ReductionFiberLemmas` (4). Counts derive from `inventory.json` and include maintained proofs, model modules and audits rather than just reusable foundations. They prioritize this sample; they are not a formal risk score or proof that these are the only high-risk modules.

A fresh Luna reviewer inspected the three index/reshape modules, definitions and selected actual consumers; another fresh Luna reviewer inspected pointwise-convolution and reduction-fiber lemmas, their definitions and selected consumers. Neither edited or built the repository. Their records are [index and reshape](independent-index-review.md) and [convolution and reduction](independent-convolution-review.md).

* `IndexMaps.lean:16–42` constructs signed-axis decoding, broadcast coordinate projection and checked static slice coordinates from range, extent and `SliceShape.IsWellFormed` premises. The model's `BroadcastAxes` checks length/range **but does not encode uniqueness or sortedness**: any particular source contract requiring those properties needs separate guards. The `broadcastView` and `sliceView` constructors at lines 44–56 are operation-specific. `IndexMapLemmas.lean` and `Theory/UnitAxisLemmas.lean` are representative actual consumers. No target rewrite equality is taken as an assumption in the inspected paths.
* `LinearIndexLemmas.lean:12–42` derives rank-general linearize/delinearize inverses from the actual recursive definitions, including scalar and empty shapes. `Theory/FiniteSum.lean:37–49` uses them to construct coordinate and count-preserving reshape equivalences. These inspected facts are generic index infrastructure; no target-equality premise was found.
* `ReshapeLemmas.lean:15–47` includes empty-tensor extensionality and composition under explicit element-count equalities; the constant-view lemma separately requires `operand.isAll value = true`. `Pilot/ReshapeRules.lean:14–37` and `ContractionRules.lean:30` consume these facts. They mix generic shape support with a specialized constant-recognition property; the count and guard premises are visible, not an assumed desired rewrite.
* `ConvolutionPointwise.lean:16–27` makes unit kernel/stride/base dilation and zero edge padding explicit; it does not assume output/input shapes are equal. `ConvolutionPointwiseLemmas.lean:29,79` derives output-shape and spatial-sample facts. `ConvolutionPointwiseAlgebra.lean:27` applies the latter to the pointwise term. This is reusable **domain-specific** infrastructure, not unrestricted convolution or a hidden arbitrary-rewrite theorem.
* `ReductionFiberLemmas.lean:27–71,139–179` proves coordinate splitting/merging, an ordered-fiber sum over reduced coordinates and a fold-map lemma with an explicit homomorphism law. It allows zero extents; surrounding `ReductionAlgebra` statements introduce their own nonempty or algebraic assumptions when required. `ReductionAlgebra.lean:97,110,122` and `WindowTransportLemmas.lean:46` are representative callers. The reviewed facts do not assume their target equalities.

The previous review separately examined explicitly rewrite-specific `ArithmeticFamilyProof`, PadWindow contribution fusion, a paper-packet signed-remainder helper and the host-specific unsigned64 diagnostic. Those are not silently reclassified as generic foundations. No demonstrated defect warrants modifying frozen source or proof semantics. Prior source/goal/axiom/replay evidence remains unchanged.

## Limits and verification

The seven prior baseline files were reviewed in full in the earlier bounded pass; the five new module reviews focus on the listed declarations, supporting definitions and selected call sites, not every theorem in each module or its full transitive proof term. Many of the other 97 modules have individual facts sampled by previous reviews, but **have not all received a full semantic review**; none are declared clean by their inventory label. The 42 mixed/unresolved role labels in particular require human inspection before stronger classification. External Lean/Std/Mathlib/FloatLib and C++ correspondence are outside this audit.

No Lean source, frozen packet, dependency pin or protected surface changed, so no Lean build/replay was run in this read-only pass. The only new checks are frozen-file hash reconciliation, lexical import extraction and manually inspected source/call-site evidence; those do not replace kernel checks. Independent index/reshape review reported approximately six minutes and convolution/reduction review approximately five minutes; they ran concurrently and cannot be added as wall time. Exact worker/coordinator active intervals and token/cost telemetry are unavailable. No fresh proof, performance or blanket cleanliness claim follows.
