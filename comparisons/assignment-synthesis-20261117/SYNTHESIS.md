## Paper comparison

[`comparisons/tensorright-v1/REPORT.md`](../tensorright-v1/REPORT.md) and its 118-row `cases.json` report 116 Lean model proofs, one model counterexample (`slice/rule13`) and one case vacuous under the executed paper model (`conv/rule01`). The archived TensorRight result reported 115 successes and three failures; its preserved independent rerun matched those 118 reported outcomes. Two prior paper failures, `divmod/rule06` and `divmod/rule07`, are among the Lean model proofs. One paper-reported success is vacuous; `dot/rule03`'s paper artifact checks an identity, while the Lean result proves intended associativity. No claim of 115 meaningful positive matches follows. A distinct archived XLA host-evaluator bug is documented in the comparison report; it was not newly reproduced in this assessment.

The prior comparison is solution-informed: authors' statements, hints and earlier project proofs were reused; case-to-theorem mappings are agent-authored judgments, not independently validated translation for every case. Historical replay records 30 modules and a 46.87-second check with standard axioms, not proof-discovery time. Our present read-only integrity check verifies 118 unique IDs, derives outcomes from rows, and matches every row's source and Lean proof file hash. It does not replay or independently re-review 118 translations. Full source/backend correspondence remains assumed rather than proved.

## Library cleanliness and burden

A `git diff` from migration baseline `f468e943febf52567b38b4839c1812eeb5f8ae5d` to the accepted dynamic-transpose revision shows no change under `TensorRewrite/`, or to `lean-toolchain`, `lakefile.toml`, or `lake-manifest.json`. This only checks that tracked shared source and dependency pins did not change on that history; it does not prove the entire inherited library free of target-specific logic. The held-out proof closures were frozen and reviewed individually. The unit-axis proof used only two frozen baseline semantic modules; the dynamic-transpose closure includes seven generic semantic modules, including the two explicitly reviewed DynamicSlice bounds facts. The five terminal proofs are housed in their case packets rather than added as covert generic library lemmas. Space-depth support artifacts remain separately preserved and are not a terminal proof.

The earlier measured paper closure was **6,966 custom Lean code lines in 109 whole modules**, including 2,168 comparison lines and 4,798 shared Pilot/Theory lines; shared modules counted once, external Lean/Std/Mathlib/FloatLib excluded. It is an upper bound on module closure, not a minimal proof. The broader historical maintained tree was 15,273 code lines, including unrelated work. [`INFRASTRUCTURE.md`](../tensorright-v1/INFRASTRUCTURE.md) records actual reuse examples, method, and limitations; no controlled library ablation establishes a size or speed benefit.

For the later five held-out cases, raw physical lines of Spec plus proof respectively are 50+38, 47+14, 83+36, 27+33, and 41+19 in table order; this simple `splitlines` count includes comments and blank lines, and is not comparable to the code-only totals above. It excludes required shared modules, tests, controls, Python and reviewers, so is not total cost. Unit-axis's separately measured lexical inventory includes 60 Spec/proof code lines, 32 baseline lines, 134 active control/operational lines and 49 archived control/failure lines. Other full attributable held-out closure sizes are not asserted; reuse overlaps across cases and inherited/support work is not free.

No comparable fixed-tactic fresh-discovery baseline, per-agent token/cost telemetry or defensible total cost is available. All failed trials remain in case packets; no speedup or causal productivity claim is justified.
